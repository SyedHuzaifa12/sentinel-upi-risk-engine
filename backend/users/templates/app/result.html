Understanding UPI frauds · types, risks & safety

There are 8 major types of UPI frauds — below is how they occur, which ones are most dangerous, and how to stay protected.

Phishing & Vishing

DANGER: HIGH (direct credential theft)
How it occurs: Fake emails/SMS posing as bank, fake customer care calls asking for PIN/OTP, or fraudulent links that clone UPI login pages.
Safe: Never share OTP/PIN. Banks never ask for them. Hang up and call official number.

Fake / swapped QR codes

DANGER: HIGH (widespread & silent)
How it occurs: Fraudsters replace legitimate merchant QR stickers with their own. User scans and pays the fraudster.
Safe: Always verify receiver name before entering PIN. Use QR code only from trusted sources.

SIM swap attack

DANGER: EXTREME (account takeover)
How it occurs: Attacker gets a duplicate SIM through fraud, intercepts OTPs, and drains bank accounts.
Safe: If mobile network goes dead unexpectedly, contact operator immediately. Use SIM lock / biometric.

Malware & screen readers

DANGER: HIGH (invisible)
How it occurs: Malicious apps (often fake “loan” or “utility” apps) gain accessibility access and capture UPI credentials.
Safe: Install apps only from Play Store/App Store. Deny accessibility permission to unknown apps.

AutoPay / e‑mandate abuse

DANGER: MEDIUM (recurring unnoticed)
How it occurs: Fraudsters create recurring mandates via deceptive clicks; small amounts deducted monthly.
Safe: Regularly check active mandates in UPI app. Revoke any unknown.

Fake customer care

DANGER: HIGH (social engineering)
How it occurs: Numbers listed on Google/Facebook for “customer support” – they ask for OTP to “reverse transaction”.
Safe: Always use official support numbers from app/website. No genuine support asks for OTP.

Public WiFi / juice jacking

DANGER: MEDIUM (data interception)
How it occurs: Unencrypted WiFi or compromised charging stations intercept UPI data during transmission.
Safe: Avoid transacting on public WiFi. Disable file transfer when charging at public kiosks.

OTP forwarding scams

DANGER: HIGH (urgent deception)
How it occurs: Fraudsters call claiming "your parcel is held" or "KYC expired" and trick you into forwarding an SMS (which contains OTP).
Safe: Never forward OTPs or SMS to anyone. Banks never ask for forwarded messages.
Golden rule: STOP · THINK · VERIFY — UPI frauds rely on urgency and secrecy. No exception.
